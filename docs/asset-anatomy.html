Asset Anatomy

Every icon in the library is one flat drawing. Anatomy is the layer that gives the pieces inside it names, so a tool can change the plate of an icon without guessing which shapes the plate is.

1 September 20264 parts, 3 tones 12 of 90 product icons annotatedWorking draft

The idea, in one figure

Word is nine shapes in one file. Nothing in that file says which shapes are the document, which are the tile, and which is the letterform. A person can see it instantly. No tool can.

The same shipped SVG four times, with everything outside one part dimmed. Anatomy is the difference between the first panel and the other three.

A part is a set of shapes that share a ramp

Not a region. This is the definition that keeps the vocabulary small, and it came out of Teams. The two head circles sit visually apart from everything else and look like they need a name of their own.

The color says so. The plate’s ten shapes run from 0.323 to 0.798 in luminance, and the heads sit inside that band rather than beside it. Two pairs repeat exactly, 0.798 and 0.701 appearing twice, because each head is built from the same three-layer recipe as the bodies below it.

plate, 10 shapes, sorted by luminance

So the test is not whether shapes touch. It is whether they have to move together when the hue changes. Recolor the plate and all ten move, or the figures come apart.

The vocabulary

Four part names, deliberately few. Every name added is a question every future asset has to answer, so the aim is to prove four is too few rather than to look for a fifth.

plate

The foundation

What everything else sits on and takes its color from. Usually several shapes deep, and the part that carries the tonal ramp.

glyph

The mark itself

The letterform or symbol that identifies the product. What survives if you throw everything else away.

badge

A secondary mark

Applied on top, modifying meaning rather than carrying it: a taskbar tile, a status dot, an overlay.

accent

Depth and articulation

Folds, highlights, shadow plates, a page edge. Present for form, not for meaning, and the first thing dropped at 16px.

A second axis, tone, records exceptions rather than depths. ramp is the default and is never written down: the shape follows its part’s hue at its own position in the ramp. shade stays neutral, for shadows that read as light rather than color. hold keeps a literal color, for brand-locked and semantic shapes. Most shapes carry no tone at all.

How it is written down

In the SVG, as an attribute on each shape. Not in meta.json as a list of shape numbers, because shape order is decided by whatever exported the file, and the next Figma re-export silently invalidates every index.

Here is Word, one shape at a time, grouped under the part each one belongs to. Nine shapes, three parts. Nothing in the file says so until the attribute does.

data-part="plate"6 shapes

The document

data-part="badge"2 shapes

The tile it sits on

data-part="glyph"1 shape

The letterform

Every shape carries the attribute; nothing carries a shape number. Shape order is decided by whatever exported the file, so the next re-export from Figma would silently invalidate an index. A name survives it.

<!-- Travels with the artwork, survives a re-render. -->
<path data-part="plate" d="M9 33.09L23 16..." fill="url(#g0)"/>
<path data-part="plate" d="M9 21.04C9 18.55..." fill="url(#g1)"/>
<rect data-part="badge" x="4" y="23" width="16"... />
<path data-part="glyph" d="M17.49 26.43..." fill="white"/>

// Six shapes, one part, one ramp. The file is still a valid standalone SVG,
// and still renders in anything that has never heard of data-part.

Addressing a part

Names are only useful if something outside the file can point at one. The address is the asset id with the part appended, and each address takes a fill, a stroke or an effect.

windowsnamespace.producticonfamily.wordasset.platepart

One address, six shapes. Not one per shape — that is the whole point of a part. The tile and the letterform are untouched, because they are addressed separately.

fillstrokeeffect

These icons have no strokes at all, so there is nothing to modify. What the address makes possible is authoring one: put a two pixel outline on the plate of every product icon and leave the glyph alone.

Why this matters here, not just to Bridge

Anatomy is Bridge’s vocabulary already, and matching it now is cheaper than inventing a second one and reconciling later. But the payoff that lands first is ours.

Customizer Live Its style pipelines infer structure by bucketing shapes on luminance, because that is the only signal in the file. One threshold has to serve both “this light shape is a detail” and “this light shape is the subject”, and it cannot. That is why Shield loses its checkmark. With parts declared, recolor the plate and leave the glyph becomes an instruction rather than a guess.
Bridge Adjacent Its icon schema carries an anatomy block of named parts today. The long-term shape is that Bridge names the parts and Expressive Assets provides the drawings, which only works if both sides use one vocabulary.

Where it stands today

  • ✓

    Vocabulary agreed. Four parts and three tones, tested against twelve icons chosen to break it, including a one-shape icon and a thirty-six-shape icon.

  • ✓

    Storage decided. data-part in the SVG, not indices in metadata.

  • ✓

    Targeting proved. Marking one part and stroking that selection works on real artwork, at every size, with no change to the source drawing.

  • ·

    Twelve of ninety annotated. The pilot group, which is also the Customizer’s default set, so a change shows up in the tool the same day.

  • ·

    The other seventy-eight. Partly detectable rather than fully manual: every plate-and-letterform icon in the library has exactly one white fill, and in every case it is the letterform.

  • ·

    Customizer reading parts. The pipelines still bucket on luminance. Nothing consumes data-part yet.

Still open

  • OwnershipIf a designer re-exports an icon from Figma, who reapplies the part names. Either the importer preserves them or they are declared upstream in the Figma layer names. This needs an answer before annotation scales.
  • GranularityGradients and masks mean one visual element is often several paths. Does data-part go on each path, or does a wrapping group own it.
  • Per sizeArtwork is redrawn per size rather than scaled. The 16px Word legitimately has fewer parts than the 48px. Is anatomy a property of the asset or of the drawing.
