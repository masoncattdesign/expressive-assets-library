Seven ways to split an icon

Every recoloring style in the Customizer starts by deciding which shapes belong together. That decision is invisible in a finished icon, because a good split and a bad split can both come out looking plausible. This page makes the decision itself visible: each icon below is painted by group membership, in colors chosen to mean nothing, so the only thing you are reading is which shapes went together.

Flat’s rule

What Flat does, and why it reads well: normalize lightness within the icon, lock white letterforms and plate rects to fixed ends, then push any shape that collided with its neighbor to the nearest free accent. Shapes drawn identically stay identical.

How to read the two numbers

Accents used is how many of the four accents the rule actually reaches, averaged over the six icons. Largest group is the share of themable shapes that landed in the biggest group. A rule that scores low on the first or high on the second is collapsing the icon, and collapse is the failure mode that becomes hard to see once color is applied: the result still looks like a themed icon, it has just quietly stopped distinguishing parts that the drawing distinguishes.

Neither number is a verdict on its own. Even steps scores perfectly on both by construction, because it forces four equal groups whether or not the artwork has four parts. What we are looking for is a rule that reaches four accents and keeps the same kind of part in the same accent from one icon to the next, which is the thing only the pictures can tell you.
